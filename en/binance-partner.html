What's specific to Binance

Binance prices each product differently — so your audience's mix, not just its size, sets your rate

This is the part that genuinely differs from OKX, and most channel comparisons skip it entirely.

Binance does not run one number across the board. Its published referral material describes an approved Spot Affiliates band of 41–50%; on futures, an affiliate base of 40%, of which 10 percentage points are ceded to the referred trader, leaving roughly 30% net to the referrer; and a standard, non-affiliate spot referral base of 20%. Three different products, three different economics.

Your audience skews…Which Binance economics you land inWhat that means in practice
Spot-heavy (holders, DCA, altcoin rotators)Approved spot affiliate bandThe widest published band — but approval is the gate, not the rate
Futures-heavy (leverage, perp scalpers)Futures affiliate base, less the referred-user shareHigher fee output per head, narrower net share of it
Mixed / no affiliate approvalStandard referral baseWhere most people actually sit, and why their numbers disappoint

Worked example, and read the assumption. Take two channels that each generate $10,000/month in Binance trading fees — identical volume, different mix. At the published spot band's low end the spot-heavy channel's gross share is around $4,100; the futures-heavy channel, at roughly 30% net after the referred-user give-back, is around $3,000. Same fee output, a spread of about $1,100/month — decided entirely by what your audience trades. The assumption doing the work here is "same fee output", which rarely holds: futures audiences usually generate far more fee volume per head, which can more than reverse the gap. The point is not that one wins — it's that on Binance you cannot answer the question without knowing your mix, whereas on OKX the same question is rate-neutral.

Under our tier none of this is a qualification problem: there is no approval gate to clear and no monthly target to defend, and you are paid from 35% — a floor, not a ceiling — settled in real time in the exchange's own back office, visible to you without going through us. What the mix still changes is your ceiling, so it is worth knowing before you plan a channel. Full numbers in the Binance affiliate commission breakdown.

FAQ

Binance partner program — FAQ

Do I have to hit Binance's affiliate volume tiers to earn the high rate?+
No. Applying to Binance's affiliate program directly usually means clearing an audience/eligibility review first, then grinding monthly referred volume to reach — and keep — the higher commission tiers, or you get downgraded. By joining under our existing sub-affiliate tier, we carry those thresholds: you earn the high split from your very first referral, with no volume quota to hit and no downgrade pressure.
What rate can I actually get — is 35% real?+
Partners start at 35% of the fees their referred users generate, settled automatically in Binance's official back end — and it rises with volume toward 40% for the top tier, with no threshold to unlock it. 35% is a floor set by our tier, not a ceiling; your exact rate is per agreement.
Is this multi-level / MLM?+
No. It is single-level: you earn only on the rebate from traders you directly refer, never from people they go on to refer. There is no downline. This also keeps it compliant with Binance's referral rules — Binance downgrades multi-level / stacked referral setups.
Do I need to trade a lot myself?+
No. You earn from the rebate generated by the traders you refer. What matters is your ability to reach real traders, not your own trading volume.
Is there a cost or deposit to join?+
Zero cost. No joining fee, no deposit. You just direct people to register.
How and when is the split settled, and can I audit it?+
There's no manual payout: the rebate is settled in real time by Binance's official back-end system and paid automatically in the original currency. You can check every referral's volume and rebate in your own Binance dashboard anytime — it never passes through us.
Can my referred users also get a rebate?+
Yes. You decide how to split the rebate pool between yourself and your users — keep more as commission, or pass more back to win them over.
