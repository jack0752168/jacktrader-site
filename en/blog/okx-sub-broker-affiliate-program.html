OKX affiliate program 2026: commission rates, tiers, and how the sub-broker level works

OKX's help centre publishes a default affiliate commission of 30% of referred traders' fees. Quant teams and high-volume traders consistently get quoted higher numbers from broker channels — 40%, sometimes more. This piece is the plain-English version of what is going on between OKX's affiliate page, the sub-broker tier above it, and the rebates that flow to traders.

Skip the theory — apply as a partner (from 35%)

The four levels of OKX referral, ranked

OKX runs more than one referral structure. From thinnest to fattest:

  1. Default referral. Any user can grab a referral link from their profile. The downstream user gets a small fee discount, the upstream user gets a token commission. Usable for casual sharing; not a business.
  2. Affiliate program. Application-based. Once approved, you earn a percentage of every downstream trader's fees — published as a 30% default that OKX reassesses monthly. The actual rate is set by your downstream tree's total fee output. Most "rebate sites" you find on Google run on this layer.
  3. Sub-broker / institutional channel. The tier above standard affiliate. Sub-brokers negotiate a custom commission band up front instead of climbing the published ladder, which is what lets an established channel pass through the up-to-40% rebate from a trader’s first fill. OKX does not publish the sub-broker band, the review criteria or the onboarding thresholds anywhere in its help centre — so if you want the exact terms, ask OKX directly, not a rebate site. What is observable from the outside: onboarding is individual rather than self-serve, and established channels get operational extras such as reconciliation data and a named contact.
  4. Market-maker program. Negotiated maker-rebate band for actual quoting desks. Effective maker fees can go negative (OKX pays you to provide liquidity). Separate from the affiliate / sub-broker stack; lives at the exchange-fee layer, not the referral layer.

JackTrader's OKX channel runs on level 3 (sub-broker) — which is why we can quote up to 40% pass-through, where an affiliate-tier site has to carve its offer out of OKX's published 30% default share.

Why the published rate is never what reaches the trader

The reason published affiliate numbers look better than what traders actually receive is operational margin. A pure rebate site on the affiliate tier needs to keep some portion of the commission to cover:

  • Settlement engineering (per-trade dashboard, reconciliation, payouts in original currency)
  • Customer support and tier disputes
  • Compliance + KYC review on rebound accounts
  • Their own paid acquisition, content, etc.

If a channel sits on a gross band around 40% and passes roughly half of it through, the trader nets about 20% — which is the range most affiliate-tier rebate sites Google ranks for "okx rebate" actually deliver.

Sub-broker channels are different because the gross share that comes in from OKX is meaningfully larger, so even after the same operational margin, the pass-through to traders can end at 40% rather than inside the 30% default band. That is the entire reason high-frequency desks bother to negotiate sub-broker terms instead of just clicking the first referral link.

What the maker-rebate side looks like on OKX

OKX runs a specific program for designated market makers. To qualify you need consistent two-sided quoting across a configurable set of instruments, with uptime and spread obligations. Effective maker fees inside that program reach −0.005% to −0.015% depending on the band. This is the closest thing the retail-accessible OKX stack has to a "we pay you to trade" tier.

You can run both. The market-maker program changes the exchange fee directly; the sub-broker rebate sits on top of whatever fee actually got booked.

Worked example — a quant team at $50M / month on OKX perpetuals

Take a quant desk doing $50M / month USDT-perpetual notional, 75% maker / 25% taker, sitting at VIP 3:

  • Maker = $37.5M × 0.0120% = $4,500
  • Taker = $12.5M × 0.0400% = $5,000
  • Gross fees = $9,500 / month
  • 40% sub-broker rebate ≈ $3,800 / month back in USDT, settled in real time
  • Annualised ≈ $45,600 / year

For the same desk with a plain affiliate-tier rebate (25%), the same volume returns ~$2,375 / month — a difference of $17,100 a year, on identical trading flow.

What stops people from negotiating sub-broker terms directly with OKX

  • Minimum volume gate. OKX won't open a sub-broker tree for an account without a credible, sustained volume profile. A single quant team at $50M / month qualifies; one casual trader does not.
  • KYB + business documentation. Sub-broker onboarding is a corporate process, not a click-through. There's a contract, an entity-side KYC, and reporting obligations.
  • Operational lift. Even after onboarding, you need to build a reconciliation system, a dashboard, a payout pipeline. Most desks don't bother — it's cheaper to join an existing sub-broker channel.

The pragmatic version: if you have the volume, run your own sub-broker tree. If you don't, plug into an existing one and pocket the difference between an affiliate-tier rebate (carved out of 30%) and a sub-broker tier rebate (up to 40%). How to evaluate an OKX rebate offer

  1. Ask which tier they're on. Affiliate, sub-broker, or institutional partner? A specific answer beats a vague one.
  2. Demand per-trade reconciliation. Anyone serious can show you the per-trade fee that came through the affiliate ledger. If they can only show aggregate monthly totals, they're hiding the math.
  3. Check the settlement currency. USDT is standard. Token rewards or campaign points are not the same thing.
  4. Check who keeps the BNB-equivalent discounts. On OKX the equivalent is the VIP-tier discount and the OKB discount — these belong to you, not the rebate provider.
  5. Check the assignment policy. Some affiliates rotate trees aggressively and your rebate rate quietly drops. Sub-brokers have to commit to a fixed band.

OKX copy-trading has its own attribution model (trader profit-share + lead-trader commission) which is separate from the spot / futures fee rebate. The trading fees paid by copiers do flow through the rebate ledger; the copy-trading profit share does not.
