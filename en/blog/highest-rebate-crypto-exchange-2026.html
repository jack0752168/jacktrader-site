Crypto exchange with the highest rebate 2026: the real ranking

The short version

Advertised rebate vs what you keep

A rebate is a percentage of a percentage. The exchange charges a trading fee; a slice of that fee goes to the affiliate or sub-broker who referred you; and the rebate is the share of that slice handed back to you. So three numbers stack underneath every rebate banner:

  1. Base fee — what the exchange charges before anything (e.g. 0.10% spot, 0.02%/0.05% maker/taker on futures).
  2. Commission rate — the cut the partner earns on your fee (this is what some sites misquote as "the rebate").
  3. Pass-back share — how much of that commission reaches you. This is the only number you spend.

"Up to 50% / 60%" almost always refers to the commission (number 2), or bundles in native-token discounts like BNB, or is a short promo. The honest figure to compare across venues is number 3 expressed against your actual fee.

Worth separating from the question this page answers: a high pass-back share is not the same thing as a low price. Number 1 in that stack — the base fee each venue charges before any rebate exists — is ranked on its own in the 2026 cheapest-exchange breakdown, and the two answers do not always point at the same venue.

Worked example: same volume, different dollars

Two traders, both doing $5,000,000 / month in USDT-M futures, 80% maker / 20% taker. The only difference is the base fee.

Extra fee vs Trader A—~$1,000
Share of fee B must get back just to match A's gross bill—~43%

Before any rebate, Trader B is already about $1,000 a month behind. B's channel would have to hand back roughly 43% of every fee just to draw level with what A pays before A's own rebate — and A can still add a rebate on top. A banner rebate on B's venue can produce the bigger dollar figure, but only because B is being overcharged at the gross level. A fat rebate on a fat fee is not a deal. How to actually get the highest rebate

  • Register through a sub-broker channel, not a plain referral. On a standard Binance referral link the 20% base commission belongs to the inviter, and the published pass-back options to you are only 0%, 5% or 10%; larger pass-backs are only possible through a higher-tier affiliate or sub-broker account.   • Stack the native-token discount. On Binance, BNB cuts the gross fee first (25% spot / 10% futures); the rebate then sits on top. They do not cancel each other — run both.
  • Confirm the terms up front. Know the rate, what it is calculated on and how it is paid before you trade. A clear, reconcilable arrangement beats a higher banner number that pays monthly or holds your rebate hostage behind a lock-up.
  • Already have an account? Binance permits a one-time official rebind when the account is unbound and its trailing 90-day trading volume is 5,000 USD or less (account age is irrelevant) — walkthrough in rebinding an existing account. Don't assume you're locked out.
  • Reconcile. A real rebate is verifiable: match every settlement against your exchange fee history. If it doesn't tie out, it isn't the highest rebate — it's the highest claim.

Is a higher advertised rebate percentage always better?+
No. A rebate is a percentage of the fee you actually pay, so a bigger rebate percentage on a high-fee exchange can still leave you paying more than a smaller one on a cheaper exchange. Always compare net effective fee — base fee, minus any native-token discount such as BNB, minus the rebate — rather than the headline rebate number on its own.
Does a rebate change the price or speed of my trades?+
No. The rebate is a cash-back on fees the exchange already charged, paid out separately — with JackTrader, the exact rate and payout method are confirmed one-on-one. Your orders, fills, leverage and account all behave exactly as they would without it. The only difference is that part of the fee comes back to you afterward.
