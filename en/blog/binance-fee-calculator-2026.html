Binance fee calculator 2026: maker, taker, VIP tier & the BNB discount stack

Binance publishes its fee schedule on three different pages, and the effective rate you actually pay depends on which side of the book you fill, which product you trade, your 30-day volume, your BNB balance, and whether you came in through an affiliate link. This guide collapses all of that into one model, so you can see what you actually pay at your volume.

The short version

Binance's published fee for a Regular user is 0.10% on spot, 0.02% maker / 0.05% taker on USDT-M futures, and a similar 0.02% / 0.05% on COIN-M futures. From there, four discounts can stack:

  1. BNB fee discount — pay fees with BNB and get 25% off on spot and 10% off on futures (subject to the promo schedule).
  2. Referral kickback — under Binance's standard referral mode the person who invited you earns a 20% base commission on your spot fees, and chooses whether to pass back 0%, 5% or 10% of it. It is the inviter's setting, not an automatic discount, so a plain link can pass back nothing.
  3. VIP tier — 30-day volume tiers cut maker / taker fees in steps from VIP 1 to VIP 9.
  4. Affiliate / sub-broker rebate — if your referrer is on an affiliate or sub-broker tier (like JackTrader's Binance channel), they can pass back part of the commission Binance pays them — how much is the referrer's call, not an automatic discount. Which tier your referrer actually sits on is set by Binance's own eligibility bar — the qualification rules behind the Binance affiliate program explain why two codes can advertise the same headline and pay out very differently.

The four discounts compound. We work out an example below.

Worked example #1 — a grid bot trader at $5M / month

Assume you run a Binance USDT-M grid bot at $5,000,000 notional volume per month. The bot is 85% maker, 15% taker (typical for a tight-grid range strategy).

  • Maker volume = $4.25M × 0.0200% = $850 in maker fees
  • Taker volume = $0.75M × 0.0500% = $375 in taker fees
  • Gross monthly fees = $1,225

Apply the BNB futures discount (10% off): $1,102.50 / month — roughly $13,230 a year in fees from a single $5M / month bot.

That annual line is what a rebate works against, and the bot pays it on every fill whether the strategy is profitable or not. For two bots at the same volume, scale linearly; once a single account clears $15M / month and meets Binance's VIP 1 criteria, the futures rates themselves step down. Worked example #2 — a high-frequency taker at $20M / month

An HFT desk hitting both sides aggressively might be 30% maker, 70% taker at $20M monthly.

  • Maker = $6M × 0.0200% = $1,200
  • Taker = $14M × 0.0500% = $7,000
  • Gross = $8,200 / month

Pay with BNB (10% off futures): $7,380 / month. At $20M the desk also clears the VIP 1 volume gate ($15M / month); if it meets Binance's VIP 1 criteria, USDT-M rates step down to 0.0160% maker / 0.0400% taker: $960 + $5,600 = $6,560, or ~$5,904 / month after BNB — still about $71,000 a year. Stacking order — what compounds with what

This is the single most common point of confusion. The discounts apply in this order:

  1. Binance computes the gross fee at your current VIP tier rate.
  2. If you have BNB enabled, Binance applies the BNB discount (25% spot / 10% futures) to that gross fee.
  3. The remaining fee is what Binance keeps and the affiliate side splits.
  4. The affiliate or sub-broker who referred you receives a commission share under Binance's official program rules, and decides how much of it to pass back to you. Through JackTrader, the rate and payout method are confirmed one-on-one.

BNB does not cancel the rebate; the rebate does not cancel BNB. You can — and should — run both. Same for VIP tier: the rebate sits on top of whatever band you're already on.

How to climb VIP tiers faster

  • Concentrate volume in fewer sub-accounts. Each sub-account has its own VIP calculation, and spreading volume across accounts often keeps you in the Regular band on each.
  • Use futures volume to gate rather than spot — the bands are easier to clear on futures notional than on spot dollar volume.
  • Hold $1k+ in BNB in your spot account (qualifies for the VIP volume + BNB criteria simultaneously).
  • Don't conflate VIP with the rebate — VIP changes the gross fee, the rebate changes what you keep after.

FAQ

Does a referral rebate still work for VIP 1+ accounts?

Yes, within Binance's limits. A rebate is calculated on whatever fee Binance actually charges you, so its dollar amount moves with your VIP rate — a lower tier rate means a smaller absolute rebate on the same volume.

What happens to my rebate at higher VIP tiers?

Binance's own rules set the ceiling: commissions stop once the referred user reaches VIP 3 or above, and Futures-account commissions are capped at 12 months per referral. That is why high-volume accounts are assessed individually. The underlying tier logic — what Binance pays an introducer and how that share moves — is laid out in the Binance affiliate commission breakdown.

Is the BNB discount going away in 2026?

Binance has rolled the BNB futures discount through several extensions. It is currently active; we update this page when the schedule changes.

How do I check my actual fee history on Binance?

Inside Binance: Wallet → Transaction History → Fee Income for spot, or your USDT-M futures statement. Those records are the baseline for any rebate: whatever a referrer pays you back should reconcile against the fees shown there.
