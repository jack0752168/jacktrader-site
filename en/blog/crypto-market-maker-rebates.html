Crypto market-maker rebates: how negative maker fees actually work

When an exchange quotes a negative maker fee, it is no longer charging you to trade — it is paying you to post liquidity. For a desk pushing nine figures of monthly volume, that sign flip turns the fee line from a cost center into a revenue line. But "negative maker fees" hides two very different things: the VIP fee ladder you climb automatically by volume, which on Binance takes the maker fee down to 0.0000% at most, and the dedicated market-maker program you apply into, post collateral for, and get measured on, which is where negative rates actually live on Binance. They are not the same product, they often exclude each other, and the way a third-party rebate stacks on top differs sharply between the two. This is the operator's map.

What a negative maker fee really is

On a normal trade you pay a fee. The maker fee is the cheaper of the two because a resting limit order adds depth to the book; the taker fee is higher because a market order removes it. A negative maker fee inverts the maker side entirely: instead of debiting your account when your resting order gets filled, the exchange credits it. The taker on the other side of your fill pays a positive fee, the exchange keeps the spread between the two, and a slice of the taker's fee is rebated back to you for having supplied the liquidity that made the trade possible.

Two things follow immediately, and both matter for how you model a desk. First, a negative maker fee only earns when your order is actually filled as a maker — an unfilled resting quote pays nothing, and a quote that crosses the spread executes as a taker and pays the positive taker rate. Fill quality, not just fee tier, drives the economics. (If post-only mechanics are still fuzzy, our maker vs taker breakdown covers how to guarantee a maker fill.) Second, the rebate is a function of maker volume, so two desks at the identical VIP tier can collect wildly different rebates depending on how much of their flow rests versus crosses.

The two ways to reach a negative maker fee

There are two distinct paths toward a zero or negative maker fee, and on Binance they end in different places. Conflating them is the single most common mistake desks make when they model fee revenue.

The decisive line is the last one. On most venues, joining the dedicated market-maker program means your fees are set by the MM agreement instead of the public VIP schedule and its promotions — not on top of them. KuCoin made this explicit in 2026 when it removed the VIP maker rebate and consolidated all maker incentives inside the market-maker program. So the question is never "can I have both?" — it is "which track pays my specific flow more?"

The dedicated market-maker program: a different animal

The VIP ladder is passive — hit the volume, get the rate. The dedicated market-maker program is an agreement. You apply, you are reviewed, and if accepted you take on quoting obligations in exchange for fees the public schedule never shows.

Binance Futures Market Maker Program. The published entry bar is 30-day volume exceeding 1,000 BTC (evidence of comparable volume on another venue is also accepted), plus "quality market-making strategies." You apply by emailing the program desk (mmprogram@binance.com) with proof of your market-making volumes, or through your Key Account Manager. Accepted makers receive negative maker fees on selected pairs, and — this is the operational catch — those negative rates are repriced on a rolling basis to reflect your market-making performance. Stop quoting tightly and your edge can be cut.

Binance.US Market Maker Program shows the mechanics in public numbers: applicants are encouraged above $10M monthly volume, qualifying participants get 0-maker pricing on select pairs, and the ranked top performers earn additional rebates — the #1 maker up to an extra 0.005%, ranks 2-5 an extra 0.002%. Enrollment rolls every Thursday. Note the structure: it is a competition, not a static tier. Your rebate depends on where you rank against other makers, not just on a volume threshold you cleared.

The takeaway for a desk: the dedicated program can beat the VIP ladder on specific pairs you are willing to quote continuously, but it comes with obligations, performance repricing, and — critically — usually replaces the public VIP pricing and shuts you out of volume and affiliate promotions. It is the right call for a committed liquidity provider on a handful of books, and the wrong call for a multi-strategy quant desk that wants flexibility across hundreds of symbols.

The honest comparison is never "negative beats positive" — it is "negative-with-obligations-and-exclusions" versus "small-positive-with-freedom." Smaller desks face a different trade-off, because below VIP 3 a referral rebate can still stack on their fees — which is the next section.

The practical sweet spot is exactly where most real desks live: anyone from Regular up through VIP 2 — under $250M of 30-day futures volume — is paying a positive maker fee, and a referral rebate is measured on every basis point of it. A desk at VIP 2 (0.0140% maker) running $80M/month at an 80% maker ratio pays roughly $8,960 in monthly maker fees, plus its taker fees; that bill is the base any rebate is measured against, and claiming it does not touch strategy, custody, or API keys. The rebate compounds with the maker discount you already earn by posting limit orders; it does not replace it. The corollary is the part to be honest about: on Binance the referral rebate stops long before the maker fee reaches zero, because commissions end at VIP 3, and Futures commissions are capped at 12 months per referral. If you are at VIP 3 or above, or inside a dedicated MM program, the rebate is no longer your fee lever — your tier and program negotiation are. For everyone below that, which is the overwhelming majority of trading desks, a referral rebate on a positive maker fee is the lower-friction move.

Existing account? You may still be eligible

A common blocker: "I already have a Binance account, so I can't bind a rebate." Binance runs a rebind path for existing accounts. You qualify if you have completed KYC, are a master account with no current referrer, have traded no more than 5,000 USD in the last 90 days, are not flagged as an abnormal or wash-trading user, and are not in a restricted region. Note the activation gate: the binding only takes effect once you trade 150,000 USD within 30 days of applying — a low-activity account is worth checking before you assume you're locked out.

How to decide, in one pass

  • Below VIP 3 (under $250M of 30-day futures volume)? Your maker fee is positive and a referral rebate can still apply. Optimise your maker ratio with post-only fills and stack a Binance rebate on top. This is most desks.
  • Committed to quoting a handful of pairs continuously? Model the dedicated MM program — but price in the quoting obligation, the performance repricing, and giving up public-tier pricing and any affiliate rebate.
  • Multi-strategy across many symbols? Stay on the ladder for flexibility; the program's per-pair obligations rarely fit diversified flow.
  • Already at VIP 3 or above? Binance's referral commission has stopped at your tier. Focus on maker ratio and BNB fee payment, and negotiate your tier or MM terms directly.

Whatever the path, reconcile your real fee history against whatever rebate you receive — the numbers should match your own records. FAQ

What is a negative maker fee in crypto trading?+
A negative maker fee means the exchange pays you instead of charging you when your resting limit order is filled as a maker. The taker on the other side pays a positive fee, the exchange keeps the spread, and a portion is credited back to you for supplying liquidity. It only earns on orders that actually fill as makers — unfilled or spread-crossing orders earn nothing.
At what volume does the maker fee go negative on Binance?+
On Binance's published USDⓈ-M futures ladder it doesn't. The maker fee steps down from 0.0200% at Regular to 0.0100% at VIP 4 (about $600M in 30-day volume) and reaches 0.0000% at VIP 8 (about $15B), where it stays at VIP 9. Negative maker fees on Binance come through the dedicated market-maker program, on selected pairs and repriced on performance. Below the top tiers, the ladder keeps your maker fee positive but reduced.
What is the difference between the VIP fee ladder and a dedicated market-maker program?+
The VIP ladder is automatic — you earn the rate by hitting a 30-day volume threshold, with no obligations. The dedicated market-maker program is an application-based agreement where you select pairs, often post collateral, and take on quoting obligations in exchange for negotiated negative fees on selected pairs that get repriced based on your performance. Crucially, joining the dedicated program usually replaces the public VIP pricing and excludes you from volume and affiliate promotions.
How do I apply to the Binance market-maker program?+
The published entry bar is 30-day volume above 1,000 BTC (comparable volume on another exchange is also accepted), plus quality market-making strategies. You apply by emailing the program desk (mmprogram@binance.com) with proof of your market-making volume, or through your Key Account Manager. Binance.US runs a parallel program encouraging applicants above $10M monthly volume, with ranked rebates for top performers.
Does a third-party affiliate rebate stack with VIP maker fees?+
Yes, on a positive maker fee and within Binance's limits. From Regular up through VIP 2 you pay a positive maker fee, and a referral rebate is measured on that paid fee, stacking on top of your BNB discount and VIP tier. Binance stops referral commissions once a referred user reaches VIP 3 and caps Futures commissions at 12 months per referral, and dedicated market-maker program participants are typically excluded from affiliate and volume promotions altogether.
Can I still get a rebate if I already have a Binance account?+
Possibly. Binance runs a rebind path for existing accounts. You are eligible if you have completed KYC, are a master account with no current referrer, have traded no more than 5,000 USD in the last 90 days, are not flagged as an abnormal or wash-trading user, and are not in a restricted region. The binding only activates once you trade 150,000 USD within 30 days of applying, so a low-activity account is worth checking before assuming you are locked out.
