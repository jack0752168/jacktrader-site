OKX fee calculator 2026: maker, taker, VIP tier & the OKB level stack

OKX splits its fees across spot, perpetual futures and options, and your effective rate depends on which side of the book you fill, your 30-day volume, your OKB holdings, and whether you came in through a sub-broker channel. This guide collapses all of it into one model — with the full tier tables, three worked examples, and where an up-to-40% rebate fits on top.

The short version

At the base Regular Lv1 tier OKX charges roughly 0.080% maker / 0.100% taker on spot and 0.020% maker / 0.050% taker on USDT perpetual futures. The single most useful thing to know: OKX spot is asymmetric — makers already pay less than takers at tier one — whereas Binance's spot fee is symmetric at 0.100%. So a maker-heavy strategy is structurally cheaper on OKX spot before any discount. From there, three things move your effective rate:

  1. Your fee level — OKX sets it from a mix of 30-day volume and OKB holdings, stepping you from Regular Lv1–Lv5 up through VIP1–VIP8.
  2. OKB holdings — unlike Binance's flat BNB cashback, OKB counts toward the criteria that lift your level, so it lowers the base rate itself.
  3. Affiliate / sub-broker rebate — a channel like JackTrader's OKX channel can pass back up to 40% of the fee you paid, settled in real time in USDT.

All three compound. We work an example below.

Worked example #1 — a grid bot trader at $5M / month

Assume you run an OKX USDT perpetual grid bot at $5,000,000 notional volume per month, 85% maker / 15% taker (typical for a tight-grid range strategy) at the Regular Lv1 rate.

  • Maker volume = $4.25M × 0.0200% = $850 in maker fees
  • Taker volume = $0.75M × 0.0500% = $375 in taker fees
  • Gross monthly fees = $1,225

Now stack the JackTrader sub-broker rebate at up to 40% — calculated on the fee you actually paid, settled in real time in USDT:

  • Weekly rebate ≈ $1,225 × 40% ÷ 4 = ~$123 / week
  • Annual rebate ≈ ~$5,880 / year from a single $5M / month bot

For two bots at the same volume, scale linearly. Push OKB holdings up to lift your fee level and the gross shrinks further — the rebate is then calculated on the smaller number, but the percentage stays the same. One caveat before you model anything: these tables only apply if you can trade on OKX's global platform at all, so check whether OKX is available where you are, including the US restrictions. Worked example #2 — a spot trader at $2M / month (the OKX maker edge)

This is where OKX's asymmetric spot fee actually shows up. A spot trader doing $2M / month, 60% maker / 40% taker at Regular Lv1:

  • Maker = $1.2M × 0.0800% = $960
  • Taker = $0.8M × 0.1000% = $800
  • Gross = $1,760 / month

The same $2M / month on Binance spot (symmetric 0.100% both sides) would cost a flat $2,000 — so OKX's tier-one maker discount already saves ~$240/month before any rebate. Add the up-to-40% rebate and roughly $704 / month comes back on top. For a maker-heavy spot desk, the venue choice and the rebate compound in the same direction.

Worked example #3 — a high-frequency taker at $20M / month

An HFT desk hitting both sides aggressively might be 30% maker / 70% taker at $20M monthly futures notional, Regular tier:

  • Maker = $6M × 0.0200% = $1,200
  • Taker = $14M × 0.0500% = $7,000
  • Gross = $8,200 / month

At $20M / month you clear the VIP volume gates, which steps the base rates down; at the up-to-40% rebate band this is roughly $3,280 / month back before the tier discount even lands. For a taker-dominated book, the taker leg is the whole game — no tier ever takes it to zero, so the rebate is the only lever that reliably compresses it.

Stacking order — what compounds with what

This is the single most common point of confusion. The discounts apply in this order:

  1. OKX computes the gross fee at your current fee level (set by 30-day volume + OKB holdings).
  2. That level already reflects any OKB benefit — there is no separate cashback toggle to apply.
  3. The remaining fee is what OKX keeps and the affiliate side splits.
  4. The sub-broker (like JackTrader) receives a share and rebates up to 40% of that back to you in real time in USDT.

OKB level does not cancel the rebate; the rebate does not cancel your tier. Run both. The rebate always sits on top of whatever band you are already on.

How to climb OKX fee levels faster

  • Use OKB to jump the Regular ladder — Lv1 → Lv5 is driven mostly by OKB holdings, so a qualifying balance can beat what your volume alone would reach.
  • Gate on futures volume — perpetual notional turns over far faster than spot, so the VIP bands clear sooner on futures.
  • Concentrate flow — spreading volume across many sub-accounts often keeps each one in a low tier; consolidating lifts the whole book.
  • Don't conflate level with the rebate — your fee level changes the gross fee; the rebate changes what you keep after.

FAQ

What are OKX's actual trading fees in 2026?+
At the base Regular Lv1 tier, OKX charges roughly 0.080% maker and 0.100% taker on spot, and 0.020% maker / 0.050% taker on USDT perpetual futures. Unlike Binance, whose spot fee is symmetric at 0.100% for both sides, OKX already gives makers a discount at tier one — so a maker-heavy strategy pays less on OKX spot from the very first trade. From there, your 30-day volume and OKB holdings move you up the VIP ladder, and a sub-broker rebate can return up to 40% of whatever fee you pay. Always verify the current numbers against OKX's official fee schedule, because the bands are adjusted periodically.
How is OKX's fee calculator different from Binance's?+
Two structural differences. First, OKX spot is asymmetric from tier one (0.080% maker vs 0.100% taker), while Binance spot is symmetric at 0.100% — so makers are cheaper on OKX before any discount. Second, OKX has no BNB-style 'pay fees with the token for 25% off' toggle; instead, holding OKB counts toward the criteria that lift your VIP level, which lowers the base rate itself rather than applying a separate cashback. Futures base rates (0.020%/0.050%) are effectively identical on both venues. The rebate mechanics are the same idea on both: up to 40% of your paid fee, settled in real time in USDT.
Does holding OKB lower my OKX fees?+
Indirectly, yes. OKX sets your fee level using a combination of 30-day trading volume and OKB holdings, so parking a qualifying OKB balance can bump you into a higher Regular or VIP level than volume alone would reach, which lowers your base maker and taker rates. That is different from Binance's BNB discount, which is a flat percentage taken off the fee at settlement. With OKB the benefit is baked into the tier, so it stacks cleanly with a sub-broker rebate — the rebate is always calculated on whatever base fee you actually end up paying. Holding a token to save on fees carries its own price risk, so size that position deliberately.
How does the up-to-40% rebate stack on OKX fees?+
The rebate sits on top of every other discount. OKX first computes your gross fee at your current tier, then a sub-broker or affiliate channel (such as JackTrader's OKX channel) receives a share of that fee and passes up to 40% of it back to you, settled in real time in USDT. It does not cancel your VIP tier and it does not require you to change your strategy — a $5M-per-month grid bot at the Regular tier pays about $1,225 in monthly fees, and a 40% rebate returns roughly $490 of that every month. The exact percentage you qualify for is reviewed per account and is a maximum reference, not a guarantee.

Inside OKX, open Assets → Order Center / Bills, or download the trading fee statement for spot and for perpetual futures separately. Your effective rate is total fees paid divided by total notional traded over the period, which is usually higher than the headline maker rate because part of your flow fills as taker.
